**Ward: Stroke Medicine | Discharged: 18 May 2025**

---

## What happened and why you were in hospital

You came into hospital on 14 May 2025 with sudden weakness on the right side of your face and arm, and difficulty speaking. These were signs of a stroke (acute ischaemic stroke). A stroke happens when the blood supply to part of the brain is blocked.

A scan of your brain showed there was no bleeding, so the team were able to give you a clot-busting medicine (IV alteplase) quickly to help restore blood flow.

The team also found that your heart was beating in an irregular rhythm (atrial fibrillation, or AF). This is something you already knew about, but it had not been treated with a blood-thinning medicine before. AF can increase the risk of a stroke, so your medicines were changed to help protect you going forward.

---

## What was done while you were in hospital

- You were given a clot-busting treatment (IV alteplase) on the day you arrived.
- You had two brain scans to make sure there was no bleeding.
- For the first couple of days, swallowing safely was difficult, so you were fed through a thin tube passed through your nose into your stomach (a nasogastric tube). Once the swallowing team (Speech and Language Therapy) checked you and confirmed it was safe to eat and drink normally again, the tube was removed.
- A blood-thinning medicine (apixaban) was started on day 4 to reduce the risk of another stroke linked to your AF.
- A cholesterol-lowering medicine (atorvastatin) was also started.
- The Early Supported Discharge (ESD) stroke rehabilitation team have agreed to support you at home.
