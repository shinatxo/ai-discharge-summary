# Your Discharge Letter

**From the team at the hospital — please read this carefully before you go home.**

---

## Why you came into hospital

You came in on 10 May 2025 with stomach pain, a swollen tummy, vomiting, and no bowel movements. Tests showed that a blockage had formed in your small bowel (a small bowel obstruction). This was caused by scar tissue (adhesions) from a previous operation on your tummy.

---

## What we did

When you first arrived, we tried to treat the blockage without an operation. We put a thin tube through your nose into your stomach (a nasogastric tube) and gave you fluids through a drip to rest your bowel.

After two days it became clear that part of your bowel was not recovering, so you needed an emergency operation (a laparotomy). During the operation the surgeon:

- cut away the scar tissue that was causing the blockage (adhesiolysis),
- removed the small section of bowel that was no longer healthy (small bowel resection), and
- joined the bowel back together (anastomosis).

To help your bowel heal, the surgeon also created a **stoma** — a small opening on your tummy wall (a loop ileostomy) that lets waste leave your body into a bag while the join inside heals.

After the operation you spent time in the High Dependency Unit (HDU) so the team could watch you closely.

By the time you left hospital on 18 May 2025, your stoma was working well, you were eating and moving around, and you were able to look after your stoma yourself.

---

## Your medicines

Please take all of the medicines listed below. Some details still need to be confirmed by your doctor — these are marked clearly. **Do not leave hospital until your doctor has completed the missing information.**

| Medicine | What it is for | Dose |
|---|---|---|
| **Warfarin** | Thins your blood (for your irregular heartbeat — atrial fibrillation) | As directed by your INR result — your doctor will confirm the dose |
| **Enoxaparin** (injection under the skin) | An extra blood-thinning injection while your warfarin reaches the right level | ⚠️ *Dose and how often — to be confirmed by your doctor before you leave* |
| **Ramipril** 5 mg | Lowers your blood pressure | One tablet once a day |
| **Metformin** 1 g | Controls your blood sugar (for your type 2 diabetes) | One tablet twice a day |

**Community stoma nurse**
