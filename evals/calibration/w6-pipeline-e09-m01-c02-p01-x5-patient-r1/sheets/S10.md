## What step 7 wrote

### why_in_hospital  (from: sections.0, sections.1)

You came in while you were in labour at 39 weeks. Your baby's heart rate monitor (CTG) showed signs of distress. Because of this, you needed an emergency caesarean section.

After the birth you had heavy bleeding. This is called a postpartum haemorrhage (PPH). It happened because your womb did not contract as it should after delivery.

### what_we_did  (from: sections.2, sections.3)

The team carried out an emergency caesarean section to deliver your baby safely.

To treat the heavy bleeding, the team gave you medicines called oxytocin and ergometrine. They also used a hands-on technique called bimanual compression. The bleeding was brought under control and you did not need to go back to theatre.

A blood test showed your blood count had dropped after the operation. To help with this, the team started you on iron tablets. You were also given a daily injection called enoxaparin to help prevent blood clots.
