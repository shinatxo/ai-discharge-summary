- Hypertension

- Type 2 diabetes mellitus

[sections.2] key_investigations
- CT abdomen: adhesional small bowel obstruction, transition point identified, no ischaemia initially.

[sections.3] treatment
Initial management was conservative with nasogastric tube and drip-and-suck. Warfarin was held and INR reversed in preparation for possible surgery.

Due to failure of conservative management, the patient underwent emergency laparotomy: adhesiolysis, small bowel resection of a non-viable segment, primary anastomosis, and formation of a defunctioning loop ileostomy.

Post-operative recovery was in HDU. Stoma nurse teaching was commenced. Metformin, ramipril, and bisoprolol were restarted on day 6. Warfarin was restarted with bridging treatment-dose enoxaparin until INR is therapeutic.

[sections.7] follow_up
- Community stoma nurse follow-up arranged.
- Surgical clinic at 6 weeks (query ileostomy reversal later).

[sections.8] gp_actions
- Monitor INR.

## What step 7 wrote

### why_in_hospital  (from: sections.0, sections.1)

You came in with tummy pain, bloating, vomiting, and not being able to open your bowels. Tests showed a blockage in your small bowel caused by scar tissue (adhesions) from a previous operation.

### what_we_did  (from: sections.2, sections.3)

We did a CT scan of your tummy, which confirmed the blockage and showed where it was.

We first tried to treat the blockage without an operation. This involved a tube passed through your nose into your stomach to drain it, and a drip to keep you hydrated. This did not work well enough, so you needed an emergency operation.

During the operation, the surgeon:
- freed the scar tissue causing the blockage
- removed a small section of bowel that was not healthy
- joined the bowel back together
- made a temporary stoma (a loop ileostomy) on your tummy wall to allow the join to heal

After the operation you were cared for in the high-dependency unit (HDU). A stoma nurse began teaching you how to look after your stoma.

Your blood-thinning tablet (warfarin) was paused before the operation and then restarted. You are also taking an injection called enoxaparin alongside it until your warfarin level is in the right range. Your other regular medicines — metformin, ramipril, and bisoprolol — were restarted on day 6.
