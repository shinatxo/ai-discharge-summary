## What step 7 wrote

### why_in_hospital  (from: sections.0, sections.1)

You came in while in labour at 39 weeks. Your baby's heart rate monitor showed signs of distress. Because of this, you needed an emergency caesarean section (LSCS).

After the birth, you had heavy bleeding. This is called a postpartum haemorrhage (PPH). It happened because the womb did not contract as it should (uterine atony).

### what_we_did  (from: sections.2, sections.3)

Your baby boy was delivered safely by emergency caesarean section.

To treat the heavy bleeding, the team used:
- medicines to help the womb contract (oxytocin and ergometrine)
- a technique called bimanual compression

The bleeding was brought under control. You did not need to go back to theatre.

A blood test on day 1 after the operation showed your blood count had fallen. Because of this, iron tablets were started.

You were also given a daily injection to thin the blood (enoxaparin). This was to reduce the risk of a blood clot, which can be higher after a caesarean section and heavy bleeding.
