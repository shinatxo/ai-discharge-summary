A defunctioning loop ileostomy was formed and the patient recovered in HDU. Stoma nurse teaching was commenced prior to discharge. Metformin, ramipril, and bisoprolol were restarted on day 6; warfarin was restarted with bridging treatment-dose enoxaparin until INR is therapeutic.

## What step 7 wrote

### why_in_hospital  (from: sections.0, sections.1)

You came in with three days of cramping tummy pain, a swollen tummy, vomiting, and not being able to open your bowels.

The team found that part of your small bowel was blocked. This was caused by adhesions (bands of scar tissue), most likely from your previous appendix operation.

### what_we_did  (from: sections.2, sections.3)

A CT scan of your tummy confirmed the blockage. It showed where the blockage was and that the bowel was not damaged at that point.

To begin with, the team treated this without an operation. A tube was passed through your nose into your stomach to drain it, and you were given fluids through a drip.

Your warfarin (a blood-thinning medicine) was stopped and its effect was reversed, in case you needed an operation.

You then had an emergency operation. The surgeon:
- released the scar tissue causing the blockage
- removed a section of bowel that was no longer healthy
- joined the bowel back together
- formed a loop ileostomy (a stoma — an opening on your tummy to allow your bowel to empty into a bag)

After the operation you recovered in the high-dependency unit.

Before you went home, a stoma nurse started teaching you how to care for your stoma.

Your blood pressure tablet (ramipril), heart rate tablet (bisoprolol), and diabetes tablet (metformin) were restarted on day 6. Your warfarin was also restarted. You are also taking an injectable blood-thinning medicine (enoxaparin) until your warfarin level is back in the right range.
