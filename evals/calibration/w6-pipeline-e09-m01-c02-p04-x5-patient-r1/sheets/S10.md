## What step 7 wrote

### why_in_hospital  (from: sections.0, sections.1)

You came into hospital in labour at 39 weeks. Your baby's heart rate monitor showed signs of distress, so you had an emergency caesarean section (lower segment caesarean section).

After the birth, you had heavy bleeding (primary postpartum haemorrhage) because your womb did not contract as expected (uterine atony).

Your baby boy was born alive and in good condition, and went to the postnatal ward with you.

### what_we_did  (from: sections.2, sections.3)

To treat the heavy bleeding, the team used:
- a medicine to help the womb contract (oxytocin)
- another medicine to help the womb contract (ergometrine)
- bimanual compression

The bleeding was controlled. You did not need to go back to theatre.

Your blood count was checked after the operation and was lower than before. You were started on iron tablets by mouth. You were also started on a daily injection to help prevent blood clots (enoxaparin).

After the operation you had no symptoms, and your observations were stable.
