- Hypertension

- CT abdomen: adhesional small bowel obstruction, transition point identified, no ischaemia initially

[sections.3] treatment

Due to failure of conservative management, the patient underwent emergency laparotomy: adhesiolysis, small bowel resection of a non-viable segment, primary anastomosis, and formation of a defunctioning loop ileostomy.

Post-operative recovery was in HDU. Stoma nurse teaching was commenced prior to discharge.

Metformin, ramipril, and bisoprolol were restarted on day 6. Warfarin was restarted with bridging treatment-dose enoxaparin until INR is therapeutic.

- Monitor INR

## What step 7 wrote

### why_in_hospital  (from: sections.0, sections.1)

You came in with:
- tummy pain that came and went
- a swollen tummy
- vomiting
- not being able to pass wind or open your bowels

The team found that your small bowel was blocked. This is called a small bowel obstruction. It was caused by scar tissue (adhesions) from a previous operation.

### what_we_did  (from: sections.2, sections.3)

You had a scan of your tummy. This showed the blockage and where it was. The scan showed no sign of damage to the bowel at that point.

At first, the team tried to treat the blockage without an operation. A tube was passed through your nose into your stomach to drain it. You were also given fluids through a drip.

This did not work, so you needed an emergency operation (laparotomy). During the operation the team:
- cut away the scar tissue (adhesiolysis)
- removed a part of the bowel that was not healthy (small bowel resection)
- joined the bowel back together (primary anastomosis)
- made a stoma — a small opening in your tummy wall — to let the bowel rest (defunctioning loop ileostomy)

After the operation you were cared for in a high-dependency unit (HDU).

Before you went home, a stoma nurse started teaching you how to care for your stoma.

A blood-thinning tablet (warfarin) was stopped and reversed before the operation. It was restarted before you left, along with a blood-thinning injection (enoxaparin) to use until your warfarin level is in the right range.

Some of your other regular medicines — a diabetes tablet (metformin), a blood pressure tablet (ramipril), and a heart tablet (bisoprolol) — were restarted on day 6.
