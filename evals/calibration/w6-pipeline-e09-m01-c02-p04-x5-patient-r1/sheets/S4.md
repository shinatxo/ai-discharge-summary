[sections.0] presenting_complaint

[sections.1] diagnosis
Primary: Bronchiolitis (clinical diagnosis, RSV season)
Secondary: RSV positive (NPA result)
Background: Born at term, no significant past medical history, immunisations up to date. No family history of atopy noted.

[sections.2] key_investigations
Day 1: Temperature 37.6, RR 58, mild-moderate subcostal recession, widespread fine creps and wheeze, sats 90% on room air. Nasopharyngeal aspirate sent Day 1; RSV positive.
Day 2: Sats 93–95% on room air, RR 44, mild recession only, feeding improving (~75% orally), NG removed.

[sections.3] treatment
- Supplemental oxygen commenced to maintain sats ≥90%.
- Nasogastric feeds commenced (IV access planned if not tolerated).
- No antibiotics given (viral aetiology).
- No salbutamol given (not indicated in bronchiolitis).
- Weaned off supplemental oxygen overnight Day 1–2; NG removed Day 2 as oral feeding improving.

[sections.7] follow_up
No routine follow-up arranged.

- fewer wet nappies

A swab was taken from the back of the nose to test for the virus.

Your child was given extra oxygen to keep their oxygen levels up.
Feeds were given through a small tube into the stomach (nasogastric tube) as your child was not feeding well enough by mouth.

No antibiotics were given, as this was a viral illness.

By day 2, your child's oxygen levels were better on room air.
The feeding tube was removed as your child's oral feeding was improving.
