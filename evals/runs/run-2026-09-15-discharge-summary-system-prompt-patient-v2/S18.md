## What Happened

You were admitted to hospital after having a seizure (a fit) at work. During the seizure your whole body shook (this is called a generalised tonic-clonic seizure). It lasted about 2 minutes. After it stopped, you were briefly confused, which is normal after this type of seizure.

This appears to have been a first unprovoked seizure — meaning there was no obvious trigger found.

---

## Tests We Did

You had blood tests, a blood sugar check, and a screen for substances in your blood. All of these came back normal. You also had a CT scan of your brain, which was also normal.

Two further tests have been arranged for you as an outpatient:

- An **MRI scan of your brain**
- An **EEG** (a test that measures the electrical activity in your brain)

You will receive dates for these in due course.

---

## Treatment

You were seen by a neurology doctor (a brain and nerve specialist). No medication was started. This is because it was your first seizure and the doctors felt the immediate risk of another one was low. A decision about whether you need any medication will be made after your outpatient tests and clinic appointment.

---

## Medicines to Take at Home

You were not taking any medicines before you came in, and you have not been given any new medicines to take home.

---

## What Happens Next

You have been referred to a **first-fit clinic** (a neurology outpatient appointment). This is where the specialist team will review your test results and discuss next steps with you. Dates for your MRI, EEG, and clinic appointment have been arranged — you will be contacted with the details.

Your **GP has been informed** about your admission. They will wait for the results of your outpatient tests before taking any further action.

- **Do not swim alone.**
- **Do not work at heights.**

- **You must tell the DVLA about your seizure.** Under current DVLA rules for a first unprovoked seizure, you must not drive for **6 months from the date of your seizure (19 May 2025)**. It is your legal responsibility to inform the DVLA.

---

If you become unwell or are worried about anything, contact your GP or call NHS 111. Call 999 if it is an emergency.
