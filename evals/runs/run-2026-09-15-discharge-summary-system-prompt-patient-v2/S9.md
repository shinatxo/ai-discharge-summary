**A note for parents and carers**

## What Was Wrong

Your daughter came into hospital on 17 May 2025. She had been very thirsty and going to the toilet a lot for about two weeks. She had also lost some weight and had been sick with tummy pain for two days. When she arrived she was drowsy.

Tests showed that her blood sugar was very high and that her body had built up harmful acids. This is called **diabetic ketoacidosis (DKA)**. It happened because she has a condition called **Type 1 Diabetes (T1DM)**. This was a brand-new diagnosis — she had not had diabetes before.

---

## What Was Done in Hospital

When she arrived, the team started treatment straight away using the hospital's DKA plan. This included:

- A drip (**intravenous fluids**) to rehydrate her carefully
- Insulin given through the drip (**fixed-rate insulin infusion**) to bring her blood sugar and acid levels down
- Extra **potassium** given through the drip, because her body needed it
- Close checks every hour — both how she was feeling and blood test results

The senior intensive care team (**PICU**) were kept informed. She was looked after in the high-dependency area of the ward.

By the second day her acid levels had come back to normal. She was then moved on to insulin injections under the skin (**subcutaneous basal-bolus insulin**), which is the type of insulin she will use at home.

From day two onwards, a **diabetes specialist nurse** and a **dietician** worked with your family. Over the next couple of days, you and your daughter received education about:

- **Carbohydrate counting** — how food affects blood sugar
- **Hypoglycaemia management** — what to do if blood sugar goes too low
- **Sick-day rules** — how to manage diabetes when she is unwell

By the time she went home, both parents and your daughter felt confident giving insulin injections and checking blood sugar levels.

---
