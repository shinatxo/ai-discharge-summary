## What Was Wrong

You came into hospital with a very serious infection in your left thigh. This type of infection is called a necrotising soft-tissue infection (NSTI). It spread quickly and made you very unwell very fast. The infection was caused by two types of bacteria — Group A Streptococcus and anaerobes.

Because of the infection, your body went into a state called septic shock. This means your blood pressure dropped dangerously low and your organs needed extra support to keep working.

During your stay, a number of other problems developed as a result of how ill you were:

- Your lungs became inflamed and needed help with breathing. This is called acute respiratory distress syndrome (ARDS).
- Your kidneys stopped working as well as they should. This is called acute kidney injury (AKI). Your kidneys have since recovered.
- Your heart developed an irregular rhythm called atrial fibrillation (AF). This is being treated with medicines.
- You developed weakness from being so unwell and spending a long time in intensive care. This is called ICU-acquired weakness.

---

## What Was Done

You were very seriously ill and needed a lot of treatment. Here is what the team did:

**Operations on your thigh**
You had four operations to clean out the infection from your thigh. The first was done on the day you arrived. Further clean-up operations were done on days 2, 5, and 9 of your stay. After this, your wound was washed out and closed. A special dressing called a vacuum-assisted closure (VAC) dressing was used to help your wound heal.

**Breathing support**
Because you were so ill, you were put to sleep and a machine helped you breathe (this is called mechanical ventilation and intubation). You were also turned face-down twice to help your lungs (this is called prone positioning). You were able to breathe on your own again by day 18 of your stay.

**Blood pressure support**
You were given a medicine through a drip to keep your blood pressure up. This medicine is called noradrenaline.

**Kidney support**
While your kidneys were not working well, you had a treatment to do the job your kidneys could not do. This is called continuous veno-venous haemofiltration (CVVH). Your kidneys recovered and this treatment was stopped on day 16.

**Antibiotics**
You were given strong antibiotics through a drip to fight the infection. These were changed as the team learned more about the exact bacteria causing your infection.

**Feeding**
While you were on the breathing machine, you were fed through a thin tube passed through your nose into your stomach (a nasogastric or NG tube). As you got better, you moved on to eating and drinking normally.

**Heart rhythm treatment**
When your heart developed an irregular rhythm (AF), the team started two new medicines to help control it (see the medicines section below).

**Rehabilitation**
Physiotherapists and occupational therapists worked with you throughout your stay to help you regain your strength and movement. You moved from the intensive care unit (ITU) to a ward on day 24 of your stay.
