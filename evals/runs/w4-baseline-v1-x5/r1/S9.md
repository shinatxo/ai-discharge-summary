# Your Child's Discharge Letter

## What happened

Your daughter came into hospital because her blood sugar (glucose) was very high and her body had built up harmful substances called ketones. This is called **diabetic ketoacidosis (DKA)**. It happened because she has been diagnosed with **Type 1 Diabetes (T1DM)** — this is a new diagnosis made during this stay.

Type 1 Diabetes means the body stops making a hormone called insulin. Insulin is needed to keep blood sugar at a safe level. This is not caused by anything you or your daughter did.

---

## What we did in hospital

When she arrived, her blood sugar was very high (34 mmol/L) and her blood was too acidic (pH 7.18). We started treatment straight away:

- We gave fluids through a drip to help her body recover safely.
- We gave insulin through the drip to bring her blood sugar and ketones down.
- We checked her blood closely every hour.
- The intensive care team (PICU) were kept informed, and she was cared for in a high-dependency area of the ward.

By the next day her blood was much better (pH 7.34, ketones 0.8 mmol/L). We then moved her on to insulin injections under the skin, which is what she will use at home.

---

## Learning to manage diabetes

From day two, a **diabetes specialist nurse** and a **dietician** worked with your daughter and your family. Before she went home, you had started a diabetes education programme. This covered:

- counting carbohydrates in food,
- what to do if blood sugar goes too low (hypoglycaemia), and
- what to do when she is unwell (sick-day rules).

By the time she left hospital, you and your daughter felt confident giving insulin injections and checking blood sugar at home.
