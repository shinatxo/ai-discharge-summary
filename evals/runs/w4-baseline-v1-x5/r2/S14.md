## What happened and why you were in hospital

You came into hospital on 14 May 2025 with sudden weakness on the right side of your face and arm, and difficulty speaking. These were signs of a stroke (acute ischaemic stroke). A stroke happens when the blood supply to part of the brain is blocked.

A scan of your brain showed there was no bleeding, so the team were able to give you a clot-busting medicine (IV alteplase) quickly to help restore blood flow.

The team also found that your heart was beating in an irregular rhythm (atrial fibrillation, or AF). This is something you already knew about. AF can increase the risk of stroke, and before this admission you were not taking a blood-thinning medicine for it.

You also have high blood pressure (hypertension) and type 2 diabetes, which are noted in your records.

---

## What was done while you were in hospital

- You were given a clot-busting treatment (IV alteplase) on the day you arrived.
- Because swallowing was not safe at first, a thin tube was passed through your nose into your stomach (nasogastric tube) so you could receive nutrition safely. Once your swallowing recovered on day 3, the tube was removed.
- A speech and language therapist (SALT) checked your swallowing and supported your recovery.
- A blood-thinning medicine (apixaban) was started on day 4 to help protect you from another stroke related to your AF.
- A cholesterol-lowering medicine (atorvastatin) was started to help reduce the risk of another stroke.
- The community stroke rehabilitation team (Early Supported Discharge team) have accepted you and will continue to support your recovery at home.
