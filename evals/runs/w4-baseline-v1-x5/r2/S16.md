## What was wrong

You came into hospital with pain in your tummy, bloating, being sick, and not being able to open your bowels. Tests showed that your small bowel (the tube that carries digested food) was blocked. ---

## What we did

When you first arrived, we tried to treat the blockage without an operation. We put a thin tube through your nose into your stomach (a **nasogastric tube**) to drain it, and we gave you fluids through a drip.

After a couple of days it became clear that the blockage was not clearing on its own and that part of your bowel was at risk. You needed an emergency operation (a **laparotomy**). During the operation, the surgeon:

- cut away the scar tissue that was causing the blockage (**adhesiolysis**),
- removed the small section of bowel that was no longer healthy (**small bowel resection**), and
- joined the bowel back together (**primary anastomosis**).

To help your bowel heal safely, the surgeon also made a **loop ileostomy** — a small opening on the surface of your tummy where waste passes into a bag instead of going through the lower part of your bowel for now. This is still in place when you go home.

After the operation you spent time in the high-dependency unit (HDU) to be closely watched. Before you went home, a stoma nurse worked with you every day until you were able to look after your stoma bag on your own.
