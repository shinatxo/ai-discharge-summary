# Your Discharge Information — Stroke Unit

**This leaflet is for you to keep at home. It explains what happened, what treatment you had, and what to do next.**

---

## What Happened

You were admitted to hospital on **14 May 2025** after suddenly developing weakness on the right side of your face, weakness in your right arm, and difficulty speaking. These were caused by a **stroke (acute ischaemic stroke)**. This type of stroke happens when a blood clot blocks blood flow to part of the brain.

A scan of your brain showed there was no bleeding, so the team were able to give you a clot-busting treatment straight away.

You also have an **irregular heartbeat (atrial fibrillation)**, which is known to increase the risk of this type of stroke. This is why your medicines were changed during your stay (see below).

---

## What Was Done in Hospital

- You were given a **clot-busting injection (IV alteplase / thrombolysis)** shortly after you arrived. This is a treatment given through a drip to help dissolve the clot.
- A second brain scan the next day confirmed there was no bleeding.
- For the first couple of days, your swallowing was not safe, so you were fed through a **thin tube passed through your nose into your stomach (nasogastric tube)**. Once your swallowing recovered on day three, the tube was removed and you were able to eat and drink normally again.
- A **speech and language therapist (SALT)** checked your swallowing.
- The **Early Supported Discharge (ESD) team** — a community stroke rehabilitation team — have accepted you. They will continue to support your recovery at home.
- The team also had a conversation with you about resuscitation. You told them you would want to be resuscitated if needed, and this has been recorded.

---
