# Your Discharge Information

## What brought you into hospital

You came in with tummy pain, a swollen tummy, vomiting, and not being able to open your bowels. Tests showed that part of your small bowel (the tube that carries digested food) was blocked. This is called a **small bowel obstruction**. Scar tissue (called **adhesions**) from a previous operation was causing the blockage.

---

## What we did to help you

When you first arrived, we put a thin tube through your nose into your stomach (a **nasogastric tube**) and gave you fluids through a drip. We hoped the blockage might settle on its own, but after a couple of days it became clear that was not going to happen safely.

You had an emergency operation (a **laparotomy**). During the operation, the surgeon:

- freed the scar tissue that was causing the blockage (**adhesiolysis**),
- removed the small section of bowel that had been damaged (**small bowel resection**),
- joined the healthy ends of bowel back together (**anastomosis**), and
- created a temporary opening on your tummy called a **loop ileostomy** to let your bowel rest and heal.

After the operation you spent time in the high-dependency unit (HDU) to be closely watched. You recovered well, started eating again, and learned how to look after your stoma yourself before going home.

---

Your GP will arrange regular blood tests to check how well your warfarin is working (this test is called an **INR**). The result will guide your warfarin dose and will also tell your GP when it is safe to stop the enoxaparin injections.
