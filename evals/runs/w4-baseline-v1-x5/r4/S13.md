## What was wrong

You came into hospital with a severe infection in the skin and soft tissue of your left thigh. This type of infection is called a necrotising soft-tissue infection (NSTI). It spread very quickly and made you extremely unwell — this is called septic shock.

Because of how ill you were, you also developed:

- A condition where the lungs struggle to work properly (acute respiratory distress syndrome, or ARDS).
- A problem with your kidneys (acute kidney injury, or AKI) — your kidneys have since recovered.
- An irregular heartbeat that started during your stay (atrial fibrillation, or AF).
- Muscle weakness that can happen after a long time in intensive care (ICU-acquired weakness).

---

## What was done

**Operations**
You had four operations to clean out the infected tissue from your thigh. After these, the wound was washed out and closed. A special dressing that helps wounds heal (a vacuum-assisted closure, or VAC dressing) was used.

**Intensive care support**
Because you were so unwell, you needed a lot of support:

- A breathing machine (mechanical ventilation) to help your lungs. A tube was placed in your airway to connect you to this machine. The tube was removed on day 18 of your stay.
- Medicines given through a drip to support your blood pressure (vasopressors).
- A kidney-support machine (continuous veno-venous haemofiltration, or CVVH) to do the work your kidneys could not do. This was stopped once your kidneys recovered.
- Being turned face-down in bed (prone positioning) on two occasions to help your lungs.
- Feeding through a tube into your stomach (nasogastric feeding) until you were well enough to eat and drink normally.

**Antibiotics**
You were given strong antibiotics through a drip to treat the infection. These were adjusted once the laboratory identified exactly which germs were causing the infection.

**Heart rhythm**
You were started on medicines to manage your irregular heartbeat (AF).

**Rehabilitation**
Physiotherapists and occupational therapists worked with you throughout your stay to help you regain your strength and movement. Your wound is still healing, and a district nursing team and tissue viability team have been arranged to help with this at home.
