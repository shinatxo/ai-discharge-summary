# Your Discharge Letter

**From the Stroke Team — May 2025**

---

## What happened and why you were in hospital

You came into hospital on 14 May 2025 with sudden weakness on the right side of your face and arm, and difficulty speaking. Tests showed you had had a stroke (acute ischaemic stroke). This means a blood clot blocked blood flow to part of your brain.

A scan of your brain showed there was no bleeding, so the team were able to give you a clot-busting medicine (IV alteplase) quickly. This is called thrombolysis.

You also have an irregular heartbeat (atrial fibrillation, or AF), which was already known. This can cause clots to form and travel to the brain. Before this admission you were not on a blood-thinning medicine for it.

---

## What was done while you were in hospital

- You were given the clot-busting treatment (alteplase) through a drip on the day you arrived.
- At first it was not safe for you to swallow, so you were fed through a thin tube passed through your nose into your stomach (a nasogastric tube). Your swallow recovered after a few days and the tube was removed.
- A speech and language therapist (SALT) checked your swallowing.
- A physiotherapy team helped you to start moving again.
- You were accepted by the **Early Supported Discharge (ESD)** team, who will continue your stroke rehabilitation at home.
- Your symptoms improved during your stay.

---

## Your medicines — please read carefully

There are some important changes to your medicines.

**New medicines to take:**

| Medicine | Dose | When | Why |
|---|---|---|---|
| Apixaban (a blood thinner) | 5 mg | Twice a day | To reduce the risk of another stroke because of your irregular heartbeat (AF) |
| Atorvastatin (a statin) | 80 mg | At night | To help protect your blood vessels and reduce the risk of another stroke |

**Medicines that continue as before:**

| Medicine | Dose | When |
|---|---|---|
| Amlodipine (a blood pressure tablet) | 10 mg | Once a day |
| Metformin (a diabetes tablet) | 1 g | Twice a day |

**Medicine that has been STOPPED:**

- **Aspirin 75 mg — please do not take this any more.** It has been stopped because apixaban now does this job and taking both together would not be safe.

You have no known allergies on record.

---

## What happens next

**Early Supported Discharge (ESD) / Community Stroke Rehabilitation Team**
The team have accepted you and will be in touch to arrange your rehabilitation at home. They will help you continue to recover.

**Stroke clinic appointment**
You have an appointment at the stroke clinic **6 weeks after you left hospital**. Details will be sent to you.

**Your GP**
Your GP will need to:
- Check and manage your blood pressure.
- Do a blood test to check your diabetes (HbA1c).

Please make sure you see your GP for these checks.

---

## Driving

You have been advised **not to drive** at the moment.

You **must tell the DVLA** that you have had a stroke. There is a minimum of **one month** when you cannot drive. Your GP or the stroke clinic can give you more guidance on this.
