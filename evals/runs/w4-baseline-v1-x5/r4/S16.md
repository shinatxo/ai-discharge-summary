## Why You Were in Hospital

You came in with pain in your tummy, a swollen tummy, vomiting, and not being able to open your bowels. Tests showed that part of your small bowel (the tube that carries digested food) was blocked. This is called a small bowel obstruction. The blockage was caused by bands of scar tissue (adhesions) — these are thought to be linked to an operation you had on your appendix in the past.

You also have an irregular heartbeat (atrial fibrillation, or AF), high blood pressure (hypertension), and type 2 diabetes. These were all managed during your stay.

---

## What Was Done

**First, the team tried to treat the blockage without an operation.** A thin tube was passed through your nose into your stomach (a nasogastric tube) to drain it, and you were given fluids through a drip. Your blood-thinning tablet (warfarin) was stopped and its effects were reversed, in case you needed surgery.

**When this did not work, you had an emergency operation (laparotomy).** During the operation, the surgeons:
- cut through the scar tissue causing the blockage (adhesiolysis),
- removed the small section of bowel that was no longer healthy (small bowel resection), and
- joined the bowel back together (anastomosis).

A temporary opening was also made on your tummy wall to let waste leave your body while the bowel heals. This is called a **loop ileostomy**. It collects waste into a bag on the outside of your tummy.

After the operation you spent time in the High Dependency Unit (HDU) to be closely watched.

As you recovered, you started eating and drinking again, and you were able to move around. A specialist stoma nurse worked with you so that you feel confident looking after your stoma at home.
