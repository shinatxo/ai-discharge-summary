## What brought you into hospital

You came in because you had been passing very dark, tarry stools (called melaena) and were sick with what looked like coffee-ground material. You also felt dizzy. These are signs of bleeding from inside your digestive system.

---

## What we found

You had a bleeding ulcer in the first part of your small bowel, just after your stomach (a **duodenal ulcer**). A camera test (called an **OGD** or endoscopy) showed the ulcer was still oozing.

We also found a germ called **Helicobacter pylori** (H. pylori) living in your stomach lining. This germ can cause ulcers.

Because of the bleeding, your blood count (haemoglobin) was low — this is called **acute anaemia**.

---

## What we did

- During the camera test, we injected medicine around the ulcer and placed a tiny clip on it to stop the bleeding. This worked well.
- You had a drip of ulcer-healing medicine (a **proton pump inhibitor**, or PPI) through a vein.
- You were given **2 bags of blood (a transfusion)** to help your blood count recover. By the time you left, your blood count had risen.
- We started antibiotic tablets to clear the H. pylori germ.
- We stopped your ibuprofen. Ibuprofen was found to be the likely cause of your ulcer, so it has been **stopped permanently**.
- We also paused your blood-thinning tablet (apixaban) because of the bleed. This will be reviewed soon (see below).
