# Your Child's Discharge Letter

**Date of discharge: 20 May 2025**

---

## What happened and why your child was in hospital

Your daughter came into hospital because her blood sugar (glucose) was very high and her blood had become too acidic. This is called **diabetic ketoacidosis (DKA)**. It happened because she has been diagnosed with **Type 1 Diabetes (T1DM)** — this is a new diagnosis made during this stay.

Type 1 Diabetes means the body stops making a hormone called insulin. Insulin is needed to keep blood sugar at a safe level. Without it, blood sugar rises and the body starts breaking down fat in a way that makes the blood acidic. This is what made her feel so unwell.

---

## What we did to help her

When she arrived, we started treatment straight away:

- We gave her fluids through a drip (a thin tube into a vein) to rehydrate her carefully.
- We gave her insulin through the drip to bring her blood sugar down and clear the acid from her blood.
- We checked her blood and monitored her closely every hour.
- The intensive care team (PICU) were kept informed, and she was cared for in a high-dependency area of the ward.

By the next day, her blood was no longer acidic and her ketones (the substances that make the blood acidic) had come down to a safe level. We then moved her on to insulin injections under the skin, which is how she will manage her diabetes at home.

A **diabetes specialist nurse** and a **dietician** worked with your daughter and your family from day two. By the time she went home, you and she were confident with:

- giving insulin injections
- checking blood sugar levels
- counting carbohydrates in food
- what to do if her blood sugar goes too low (hypoglycaemia)
- what to do when she is unwell (sick-day rules)

1. **A background (basal) insulin** — given once or twice a day to keep blood sugar steady between meals.
2. **A mealtime (bolus) insulin** — given before meals to cover the carbohydrates she eats.
